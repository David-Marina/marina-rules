# E-0002 — adjusted prices rounded to ¥0.01 fabricate limit breaches below ¥1

**Symptom.** A forward-adjusted daily series shows moves of +6.17%, −6.25%, +6.12% on a security under
a 5% band (`sz.000908`, *ST, June–July 2024). The exchange's unadjusted prices for the same days are
1.19→1.25 (+5.04%), 0.93→0.88 (−5.38%), 0.72→0.76 (+5.56%) — each close **is exactly the limit price**
`round_half_up(preclose × (1 ± 5%), 2)`. Nothing illegal traded.

**Cause.** Adjustment multiplies prices by a factor < 1 and the result is stored with two decimals.
Below ¥1 the rounding step is a large fraction of the price: at preclose 0.81 the widest close that
fits in a 5% band after rounding is 0.85 (+4.94%); the series holds 0.86 because the *adjusted* 1.25
rounded to 0.86 while the *adjusted* 1.19 rounded to 0.81. Also note the column named `raw_close` in
the same file holds the adjusted value — a column name is not evidence of what it contains.

**Detector.** `python errata/E-0002/reproduce.py <adjusted.csv> [<unadjusted.csv>]` prints the
side-by-side and the limit prices.

**What to do.** Test bands on unadjusted prices (the exchange applies them to unadjusted prices), or
carry the adjustment factor and apply it to the *limit price*, not to each close. For audits, do not
widen tolerance to make these pass; mark them and keep them visible. `scripts/audit_bars.py` leaves
them red on purpose.

**Scope.** Only securities trading near ¥1 — in A-shares that is the par-value-delisting fringe, a
handful of names per year — but those are exactly the names where limit mechanics are most studied.

Samples: [`E-0002/sz.000908_adjusted_2024-06.csv`](E-0002/sz.000908_adjusted_2024-06.csv) (maintainers'
adjusted series) and [`E-0002/sz.000908_unadjusted_baostock_2024-06.csv`](E-0002/sz.000908_unadjusted_baostock_2024-06.csv)
(baostock, `adjustflag=3`).
